## Why Gestatio

In Benin, maternal and newborn mortality remains a major public health challenge: the 2018 Demographic and Health Survey recorded 391 maternal deaths per 100,000 live births, and 30 newborn deaths per 1,000 births. Health workers are few compared with the needs.

Most of these deaths are preventable. They are often explained by **three delays**: deciding to seek care, reaching care, and receiving appropriate care. Gestatio was designed to act on each of them. The goal is not to replace health workers, but to direct their attention to the women who need it most.

## The vision

An ecosystem that brings together, around the expectant mother, everyone who matters in her pregnancy: midwife, gynaecologist, paediatrician, the father or a trusted relative. It makes it possible to:

- **follow** each pregnancy continuously, until the child is 18 months old;
- **detect** risk early, in line with international medical guidelines;
- **alert the right health worker** at the right time, without any alert getting lost;
- **involve the family**, prepared to recognise danger and react.

## The principles guiding the design

- **Leave no woman behind**: the system is also designed for women without a smartphone, without network coverage or with limited literacy.
- **Women stay in control of their data**: consent, restricted access and transparency are built in from the start, in line with Beninese law.
- **Medical rigour validated by professionals**: Gestatio supports decisions and never replaces clinical judgement. Its rules are set with health workers.

## Where the project stands

Gestatio is in design, in consultation with health professionals. The first development work is under way, carried out with the care a health tool demands.

## What this project teaches me

**Rigour.** A tool that people's health depends on leaves no room for approximation. Every decision is thought through, justified and built to last.

**Empathy as a design method.** Many future users are not used to smartphones, and some have limited literacy. Every interface choice therefore starts with one question: how will this person, in her reality, understand and use this screen? It is deep work, and it is profoundly changing the way I design.
